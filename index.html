    Frequently Asked Questions

    Interior design focuses on finishes, furniture, lighting, and color within an existing shell. Interior architecture involves structural changes — moving walls, altering ceiling heights, and reconfiguring spaces. Both are essential, but they operate at different scales and require different expertise.

    Start with neutrals that have warm or cool undertones depending on your light. Add one or two accent colors you genuinely love, and use the 60-30-10 rule: 60% dominant, 30% secondary, 10% accent. Avoid trends in fixed elements like tiles and cabinetry; use them in textiles and accessories instead.

    The biggest ones are ignoring circulation paths, pushing furniture against walls, underestimating the impact of scale, and failing to define zones in open plans. Always measure, tape out furniture footprints, and prioritize movement over filling space.

    It's arguably the single most transformative element. Layered lighting — ambient, task, and accent — creates depth, supports function, and changes mood. Plan lighting early, before electrical rough-in, and use dimmers wherever possible.

    Look for materials with low embodied carbon, recycled content, and local sourcing. Reclaimed timber, hempcrete, recycled steel, and clay plaster are excellent choices. Also consider durability — a material that lasts 50 years is inherently more sustainable than one replaced every decade.

    Yes, but with intention. The key is to anchor the space with a consistent architectural shell and then layer in contrasting furniture or art. Keep proportions and materials related to maintain cohesion. Mixing works best when one style dominates and the other accents.

    About Crovelle Living Forms

    Crovelle Living Forms is an independent editorial publication dedicated to the study and appreciation of interior design and architecture. Founded in 2018, we publish in-depth articles, practical guides, and critical essays that explore how we shape the spaces we inhabit — and how they shape us.

    Our editorial team is based across Europe and North America, drawing on backgrounds in architecture, interior design, journalism, and material science. We believe that good design writing should be accessible, rigorous, and beautiful.

    Our Mission

    We exist to bridge the gap between professional practice and public understanding. Too much design media is either superficial or inaccessible. Crovelle aims to be different: we explain principles, evaluate materials, and analyze spaces with clarity and depth.
